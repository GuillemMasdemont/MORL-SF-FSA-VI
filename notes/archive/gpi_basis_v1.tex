\begin{abstract}
SF-FSA-VI \cite{kuric} plans over exit states with GPI over a policy basis and is globally optimal when the
basis is a convex coverage set (CCS). In deterministic domains with terminal exits the $d$ extrema policies
already make GPI optimal; in stochastic domains the CCS explodes (181 policies on the strong-wind double
slit, more than a thousand on a slippery Office at $\epsilon=10^{-3}$), yet GPI over a much smaller basis is
already (near-)optimal. The mismatch is that SFOLS \cite{alegre22} and GPI-LS \cite{alegre23} choose
queries and stop according to the \emph{best single policy}, while SF-FSA-VI uses \emph{GPI}. These notes
(i) characterise when GPI is optimal without a CCS, (ii) show that GPI's loss over a whole region of
weights can be certified by checking finitely many weights, and (iii) give an algorithm that queries where
GPI is worst, answers queries with metapolicies (Certify-or-Grow) whenever possible, and stops when GPI is
certified $\epsilon$-optimal.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setting}
Tabular MDP with vector features $\vphi(s,a,s')\in\mathbb R^d$, weights $\w$ in the simplex, discount
$\gamma$; terminal transitions remove probability mass, so $V^\pi_\w=(I-\gamma P_\pi)^{-1}r_\pi$.
A pool $\Pi=\{\pi_1,\dots,\pi_k\}$ with action successor features $\vpsi_j(s,a)$ defines the GPI estimate
$\hat Q_\w(s,a)=\max_j \vpsi_j(s,a)\cdot\w$ and the GPI policy $\pi^{G}_\w(s)\in\argmax_a \hat Q_\w(s,a)$.
GPI guarantees $Q^{\pi^G_\w}_\w\ge \hat Q_\w$ \cite{barreto17}. The \emph{relevant states} $\mathcal R$ are
where SF-FSA-VI reads values: the start state and the exits.
The quantity we want small is the \emph{GPI loss} $L(\w)=\max_{r\in\mathcal R}\,[V^*_\w(r)-V^{\pi^G_\w}_\w(r)]$.

\section{When is GPI optimal without a CCS?}

\begin{proposition}[Action-gap condition]\label{prop:gap}
Fix $\w$ and a state $s$ with optimal action $a^*$ and action gap
$\Delta(s)=Q^*(s,a^*)-\max_{b\notin\argmax Q^*(s,\cdot)}Q^*(s,b)$. If the GPI estimate of the optimal
action is accurate within the action gap, $c(s):=Q^*(s,a^*)-\hat Q(s,a^*)<\Delta(s)$, then GPI picks an
optimal action at $s$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For any non-optimal $b$: $\hat Q(s,b)\le Q^*(s,b)\le Q^*(s,a^*)-\Delta(s)<Q^*(s,a^*)-c(s)=\hat Q(s,a^*)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Regret controlled by coverage at the optimal action]\label{prop:cov}
For every start state $s_0$,
\[ V^*(s_0)-V^{\pi^G}(s_0)\;\le\;\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\,\mathbb E_{s\sim d^{\pi^G}_{s_0}}\big[c(s)\big],
\qquad c(s)=Q^*(s,\pi^*(s))-\max_j\vpsi_j(s,\pi^*(s))\cdot\w .\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Performance difference: $V^*(s_0)-V^{\pi^G}(s_0)=\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\mathbb E_{d^{\pi^G}}[V^*(s)-Q^*(s,\pi^G(s))]$,
and $Q^*(s,\pi^G(s))\ge\hat Q(s,\pi^G(s))\ge \hat Q(s,a^*)=Q^*(s,a^*)-c(s)$.
\end{proof}

\noindent
Only the optimal action matters, each state may be covered by a \emph{different} base policy (the max over
$j$), and errors only matter up to the action gap. The CCS asks for one policy whose value is exact at every
weight -- sufficient but far from necessary. Exactness is expensive precisely at near-indifference states
(tiny $\Delta$), where a wrong choice costs at most $c(s)$: $\epsilon$-optimality is cheap. Base policies
need not be optimal for any weight.

\section{Certifying GPI over a whole region of weights}

\begin{lemma}[GPI cells]
The weight simplex is a finite union of convex polyhedra (cells) on each of which $\pi^G_\w$ is one fixed
policy.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
At state $s$, the region where the line $\vpsi_j(s,a)\cdot\w$ is (one of) the maximum among all $k|A|$
lines is a polyhedron; intersecting one such region per state gives finitely many polyhedra on which the
GPI action at every state is fixed.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Two convex loss bounds]\label{prop:bounds}
Let $\pi$ be a fixed policy and $g_\w(s)=\max_a\vpsi^\pi(s,a)\cdot\w-\vpsi^\pi(s,\pi(s))\cdot\w\ge0$ its
Bellman gap. Let $\mathcal L_t(r)$ be the states reachable from $r$ in exactly $t$ steps under some policy.
\begin{enumerate}
\item (reachability-discounted certificate) $V^*_\w(r)-V^\pi_\w(r)\le
B^{\mathrm{reach}}_\w(r):=\sum_{t\ge0}\gamma^t\max_{s\in\mathcal L_t(r)}g_\w(s)$;
\item (OLS / chord bound) if $V^*_{\w_i}(r)$ is known exactly at weights $\w_i$, then
$V^*_\w(r)\le U_\w(r):=\min\{\sum_i\lambda_iV^*_{\w_i}(r):\sum_i\lambda_i\w_i=\w,\lambda\ge0\}$.
\end{enumerate}
Both $B^{\mathrm{reach}}_\w(r)$ and $U_\w(r)-V^\pi_\w(r)$ are convex in $\w$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(1) $V^*-V^\pi=\sum_t\gamma^tP_{\pi^*}^tA$ with $A(s)=Q^\pi(s,\pi^*(s))-V^\pi(s)\le g(s)$; row $r$ of
$P_{\pi^*}^t$ is a sub-probability supported on $\mathcal L_t(r)$. (2) $V^*_\w(r)=\max_\pi\vpsi^\pi(r)\cdot\w$
is convex in $\w$. Convexity: $g_\w(s)$ is a max of linear functions minus a linear function; maxima and
nonnegative sums preserve convexity; $U$ is the value of a parametric LP in its right-hand side (convex) and
$V^\pi_\w$ is linear.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Vertex certification]\label{thm:vertex}
On a GPI cell $C$ with policy $\pi_C$, for every $\w\in C$ and $r\in\mathcal R$,
\[ V^*_\w(r)-V^{\pi^G_\w}_\w(r)\;\le\;\min\Big(\max_{v\in\mathrm{vert}(C)}B^{\mathrm{reach}}_v(r),\;
\max_{v\in\mathrm{vert}(C)}\big[U_v(r)-V^{\pi_C}_v(r)\big]\Big). \]
Hence, if this is $\le\epsilon$ for every cell, GPI is $\epsilon$-optimal at the relevant states for
\emph{every} weight. Splitting a cell into sub-polytopes and applying the bound to each piece is also valid
and only tighter.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A convex function on a polytope attains its maximum at a vertex; apply Proposition~\ref{prop:bounds} with
$\pi=\pi_C$, which is the GPI policy on all of $C$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Along a segment of weights (always the case for $d=2$, and for every edge of the simplex) the cells are
intervals whose endpoints are breakpoints of upper envelopes of lines, so certification is exact and cheap.
In higher dimensions cells can be built lazily: a region where, at every state, one (policy, action) pair
dominates all pairs with other actions at all vertices has a fixed GPI policy (each comparison is linear).
On a cell boundary GPI's tie-breaking may mix neighbouring cells' actions; we define GPI there by a
neighbouring cell's policy, which is certified on the closed cell.
\end{remark}

\section{Algorithm}
\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{GPI-targeted Certify-or-Grow}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State $\Pi\gets\{\pi^*_{\mathbf e_1},\dots,\pi^*_{\mathbf e_d}\}$, base $\mathcal B\gets\Pi$, known values at the extrema
\Loop
  \State compute GPI cells of $\Pi$ (exact on segments; candidate weights in higher $d$)
  \State bound every cell with Theorem~\ref{thm:vertex}; \textbf{if} all $\le\epsilon$ \textbf{then return} $\Pi$
  \State $\w\gets$ the evaluated point with the largest pointwise bound
  \State $(\pi_{\mathrm{meta}},\mathrm{gap})\gets$ metapolicy over $\mathcal B$ at $\w$ (restricted PI, warm-started at GPI)
  \If{$\mathrm{gap}=0$} $\Pi\gets\Pi\cup\{\pi_{\mathrm{meta}}\}$ \Comment{optimal at $\w$, no new policy learned}
  \Else\ solve $\w$ (warm-started), add the policy to $\Pi$ and $\mathcal B$
  \EndIf
\EndLoop
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\noindent Differences with SFOLS/GPI-LS: (where) queries are the worst points of \emph{GPI's} loss, located at
GPI cell vertices, not corners of the single-policy envelope; (when) it stops when GPI is certified, not
when the CCS is complete; (how) queries are answered by metapolicies whenever certified, so most pool
members are never learned from scratch.

\subsection*{Demand-driven certified SF-FSA-VI}
For one given task, only the weights the planner actually produces matter.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Certified SF-FSA-VI (pool $\Pi$ and base $\mathcal B$ shared across tasks)}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Loop
  \State plan: $w_j(u)\gets 1$ if $\varepsilon_j$ ends the task, $V^{\pi^G_{w(v)}}_{w(v)}(\varepsilon_j)$ if it leads to $v$, else 0
  \Comment{exit values = \emph{evaluated} GPI values}
  \State $\mathcal U\gets\{u:\ \text{Bellman gap of }\pi^G_{w(u)}\text{ at }w(u)>0\}$
  \If{$\mathcal U=\emptyset$} \Return the GPI policies $\pi^G_{w(u)}$ \EndIf
  \For{$u\in\mathcal U$} Certify-or-Grow at $w(u)$ (metapolicy over $\mathcal B$; full solve only if its certificate fails) \EndFor
\EndLoop
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{proposition}
When Algorithm~2 returns, the planned weights equal the optimal ones and the composed policy is optimal for
the task (under the same convention and contraction assumption as SF-FSA-VI \cite{kuric}).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A zero Bellman gap means $\pi^G_{w(v)}$ is optimal for $w(v)$, so every planned entry is
$w_j(u)=V^*_{w(v)}(\varepsilon_j)$: the weights satisfy the optimality equations of SF-FSA-VI, whose operator is a
contraction with a unique fixed point $W^*$. Termination: GPI over a pool that contains a policy optimal for
$w$ is itself optimal for $w$ (it dominates every pool policy), hence has zero Bellman gap; so a weight is
uncertified only if no pool policy is optimal for it, and Certify-or-Grow then adds a \emph{new} policy. The
pool therefore grows in every round that does not return, and there are finitely many deterministic policies.
\end{proof}

\noindent In a model-free implementation the evaluated GPI values and the GPI check are replaced by the
metapolicy's learned SFs and its sample-based certificate (Certify-or-Grow at every planned weight).

\section{Experiments (exact, model-based)}
All numbers are exact: transition models are known, policies are solved by policy iteration, ``full
solves'' count policies learned from scratch and ``metapolicies'' count queries answered by a certified
metapolicy. Losses are measured exactly on fine weight grids or held-out weights; the code is in
\texttt{SFOLS/gpi\_basis/} and the tables in \texttt{SFOLS/18\_gpi\_targeted\_basis.ipynb}.

\paragraph{Double slit (wind $\pm3$, $\gamma=0.9$, CCS of 181 policies).}
SFOLS completes the CCS after 203 queries (all full solves). Our certificate, evaluated along each method's
pool, proves that GPI's loss at the start is below $10^{-4}$ ($\approx0.1\%$) for every weight after 21 queries
for both SFOLS' and our ordering, and below $10^{-6}$ after 109 queries for ours vs.\ 165 along SFOLS' pool.
With Certify-or-Grow every variant learns only 11--13 policies from scratch. In this 1-D problem SFOLS'
corners lower GPI's \emph{true} loss as fast as GPI-targeted queries; the gain is in knowing when to stop.

\paragraph{Slippery Office, symbol edges (out-degree-1 tasks).}
$p=0.1$: SFOLS needs 199 queries (199 full solves) for the CCS of the three edges; GPI over its pool is certified
$\le10^{-4}$ only after 105 queries and never $\le10^{-6}$. Targeted + Certify-or-Grow certifies $10^{-4}$ after 65
queries and $10^{-6}$ after 113, with 16 full solves, and reaches true loss $\le10^{-6}$ after 65 queries (SFOLS:
155). $p=0.3$: SFOLS' CCS has 346 policies; targeted + CoG certifies $10^{-6}$ after 158 queries (14 full solves).

\paragraph{Slippery Office, full simplex (held-out weights, no certificate).}
SFOLS' corners do not reduce GPI's worst-case loss for 30--40 queries ($p=0.1$: $3.2\cdot10^{-3}$ from 6 to 30
queries, $5.6\cdot10^{-4}$ after 150). Ranking candidates by the loss bound (targeted + CoG) gives $1.1\cdot10^{-3}$
after 10 queries and $1.0\cdot10^{-4}$ after 150 (24 full solves); querying the weight where GPI is truly worst
among random candidates (``GPI-greedy'', which plans at the candidates and is therefore model-based) gives
$4.2\cdot10^{-4}$ after 15 queries and $3.0\cdot10^{-6}$ after 150 (19 full solves). At $p=0.3$ the worst case decays
slowly for all methods (after 150 queries: SFOLS $1.4\cdot10^{-3}$, targeted $1.2\cdot10^{-3}$, GPI-greedy
$4.3\cdot10^{-4}$), while the mean loss is $4.8\cdot10^{-5}$, $1.5\cdot10^{-5}$ and $2.2\cdot10^{-6}$. A metapolicy
\emph{probe} rule (largest guaranteed loss $V_{\mathrm{meta}}-V^{\pi^G}$) fails on the worst case: it keeps
polishing small improvements where the base can be recombined and never visits weights where it lacks actions.

\paragraph{Windy two-stage domain.} Stage 1 is a double slit to exit A or B; calm corridors lead from A/B to
further exits. The paper's planning over the 6 corner policies loses up to 1.2\% on 11 of 60 two-stage tasks;
on the hedging edge $\{A,B\}$ SFOLS' CCS has 132 policies, while targeted + CoG certifies $10^{-4}$ after 29 queries
(16 full solves in total).

\paragraph{Demand-driven certified SF-FSA-VI.}
On a stream of 103 FSA tasks (out-degree 1 to 3) the certified planner is exactly optimal on every task and
ends with a pool of 24 policies ($p=0.1$; 13 learned from scratch) or 31 ($p=0.3$; 15 from scratch), while the
$\epsilon=10^{-3}$ CCS of the full simplex has more than a thousand policies. On double slit, a stream of 300
random weights is answered exactly with 48 policies (13 from scratch); after the first 100 weights GPI over
the pool is already certified optimal at more than 90\% of new weights. On the windy domain, the 60 two-stage tasks
are solved exactly with a pool of 8 policies (the 6 corners plus 2).

\paragraph{Query rules on segments.} Selecting by the certified bound proves $\epsilon$-optimality fastest
(double slit: $10^{-6}$ after 109 queries); selecting by the chord bound alone lowers the true loss fastest
(33 queries to $10^{-6}$) but certifies later (141); alternating between them gives neither.

\section{Limitations}
Everything is model-based: the certificates evaluate GPI policies with the model, and GPI-greedy plans at
candidate weights. Certificates are conservative (about $20\times$ on double slit; up to $1/(1-\gamma)$ when
$\gamma\to1$, because a policy that never terminates can accumulate gap). Certifying every weight needs GPI's
cells, which is cheap along segments and impractical on the full 6-D simplex. GPI's executed value is not
monotone in the pool (observed: a face loss rose from $4\cdot10^{-4}$ to $1.6\cdot10^{-3}$ after one addition).
